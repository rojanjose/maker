## How we chose {#how-we-chose}

Every machine ran the same gauntlet: a calibration cube, an overhang torture
test, a two-hundred-gram functional bracket and a forty-hour endurance queue.
We scored print quality, setup time, noise and how each printer behaved when
something went wrong — because something always goes wrong. The full rubric
lives in [How We Test](how-we-test.html).

Prices are street prices at the time of the last update. This guide is
refreshed periodically; the **Updated** date above tells you when we last
checked our picks against the market.

## FDM printers {#fdm}

<figure>
  <img src="../assets/images/fdm-printer.svg" alt="Illustration of an FDM nozzle laying down layers">
  <figcaption>FDM machines melt filament and build parts layer by layer. <span class="credit">Illustration: Makers Manual</span></figcaption>
</figure>

Fused-deposition machines are the workhorses of the hobby: cheap to run,
tolerant of abuse and happy printing brackets, enclosures and full-size props.
If you mostly print functional parts, start here.

### What to look for

- **Auto bed leveling** — non-negotiable in 2026; manual leveling is where beginners quit.
- **Build volume** — 220&nbsp;mm cubed covers most projects; buy bigger only if you know why.
- **Enclosure** — needed for ABS/ASA; skippable if you'll stick to PLA and PETG.

## Resin printers {#resin}

<figure>
  <img src="../assets/images/resin-printer.svg" alt="Illustration of a resin printer lifting a print from the vat">
  <figcaption>Resin printers cure liquid photopolymer against a screen, one whole layer at a time. <span class="credit">Illustration: Makers Manual</span></figcaption>
</figure>

Resin (MSLA) printers trade convenience for detail. Surface finish is in a
different league — layer lines effectively disappear — but you'll be washing
prints in alcohol and curing them under UV. Budget for gloves, ventilation and
a wash-and-cure station alongside the printer itself.
